(1) Introduction 
 
Project-Based Learning in Engineering Education 
Project-based learning holds an important place in engineering education because it provides students 
with opportunities to integrate theoretical knowledge with practical application while addressing 
realistic and often open-ended problems. It enables students to develop not only technical knowledge 
and problem-solving skills, but also important professional capabilities such as teamwork, 
communication, project management, decision-making, and independent learning. 
 
Unlike lecture-based courses, where much of the required knowledge and techniques may be 
introduced first through lectures, tutorials, or laboratory exercises before being assessed, project-
based learning requires students to take a more active role in determining what they need to learn and 
how to apply that knowledge. 
 
Students should therefore expect to encounter problems, technologies, methods, tools, or technical 
knowledge that have not been directly taught in previous courses. Identifying knowledge gaps, 
locating relevant information, learning unfamiliar concepts or skills, evaluating potential approaches, 
and applying newly acquired knowledge to the project are important parts of the learning process. 
 
The project supervisor provides guidance, feedback, and academic supervision. However, students 
are expected to progressively develop greater independence in investigating problems, making 
appropriate engineering decisions, overcoming technical difficulties, and evaluating the effectiveness 
of their chosen approaches. 
 
Project-based learning also emphasises the development process rather than solely the final product. 
Students are expected to demonstrate a logical progression from identifying and understanding a 
problem through investigation, design, and development to testing, evaluation, refinement, and 
professional communication of the resulting work. 
 
CCIT4080 as the Final Year Project 
CCIT4080 Project on Knowledge Products Development is regarded as the Final Year Project of 
the Associate of Engineering (AENG) programme. 
 
The assessments in this course are therefore intended to demonstrate the comprehensive knowledge 
and skills accumulated in previous courses, as well as new knowledge and skills acquired 
independently during the project. 
 
Students should expect the academic and technical standards of this course to be comparatively high.

The project should demonstrate not merely completion of assigned tasks, but also the ability to 
integrate knowledge, investigate unfamiliar problems, make appropriate engineering decisions, 
develop and evaluate solutions, and communicate the resulting work professionally. 
 
The course is intended to prepare students for further education and professional careers, where 
autonomy, the ability to address complex challenges, independent learning, engineering judgement, 
and the ability to meet high standards are essential. 
 
Students are expected to approach their project diligently, apply their accumulated knowledge 
appropriately, acquire additional knowledge and skills where necessary, and demonstrate the ability 
to address real-world engineering problems. 
 
As CCIT4080 forms part of the graduation requirements of the AENG programme, failing this 
course generally means the student has not fulfilled the graduation requirements. 
 
Course Aims and Objectives 
The course aims to provide students with an opportunity to integrate and apply knowledge and skills 
acquired throughout the AENG programme while developing greater independence in engineering 
problem-solving and project work.  
 
In particular, the course aims to: 
– Enhance Understanding: Deepen students' comprehension of the intricate relationships 
between engineering solutions and real-world problems. 
– Integrate Theory and Practice: Enable students to apply theoretical frameworks and empirical 
methods in engineering problem-solving. 
– Develop Problem-Solving and Engineering Judgement: Develop students' ability to define 
problems, investigate possible solutions, compare alternatives, make appropriate technical 
decisions, and justify the approaches adopted. 
– Promote Initiative and Autonomy: Provide an environment in which students take increasing 
responsibility for project planning, technical investigation, learning, decision-making, and 
execution under the guidance of a project supervisor. 
– Develop Project and Teamwork Skills: Strengthen students' abilities in project planning, time 
and resource management, teamwork, coordination, responsibility allocation, and professional 
communication. 
– Develop Evaluation and Reflective Skills: Enable students to test and evaluate project 
outcomes, identify limitations, consider possible improvements, and critically reflect on both the 
project process and their own development.

By the end of the course, students should be able to define an appropriate project scope, establish 
meaningful objectives and requirements, develop and implement suitable approaches, produce and 
evaluate a technically sound prototype or solution, and communicate the project process and 
outcomes professionally. 
 
Independent Project Work 
Students should not limit their project work to the designated project sessions. The scheduled sessions 
provide opportunities for group collaboration, project development, supervisor consultation, progress 
review, and use of relevant facilities; they do not represent the total amount of time expected for 
completion of the project. 
 
Students are expected to undertake substantial project work independently outside the scheduled 
sessions. Depending on the nature of the project, this may include background research, technical 
study, system design, programming, hardware construction, experimentation, testing, data collection, 
troubleshooting, documentation, group meetings, and preparation of assessment materials. 
 
The supervisor's role is primarily advisory and supervisory rather than instructional. Students should 
not expect the supervisor to provide step-by-step solutions to technical problems or to teach all 
knowledge required for the project. Where unfamiliar problems or technologies are encountered, 
students are expected to investigate appropriate sources, acquire the necessary knowledge or skills, 
explore possible solutions, and seek supervisory advice where appropriate. 
 
Students should therefore manage their time throughout the two-semester project period and maintain 
continual progress. A substantial project cannot reasonably be completed only during the scheduled 
project sessions or through concentrated work immediately before assessment deadlines.

(2) Project Description 
 
CCIT4080 Project on Knowledge Products Development is a two-semester project course forming 
an integral part of the final year of the Associate of Engineering (AENG) programme. It serves as a 
culmination of students' academic and practical learning by requiring them to integrate knowledge 
and skills acquired throughout the programme and apply them to a substantial engineering project. 
 
Nature and Scope of the Project 
The project should address a clearly identified real-world problem, engineering need, or 
technically meaningful challenge. Depending on the project scope and objectives, the proposed 
solution may involve hardware, software, or an integrated hardware-and-software system. 
 
Research-oriented projects that investigate or develop knowledge within an engineering-related area 
may also be considered. However, the project must involve substantive technical work appropriate to 
the course. A literature review alone is not sufficient. Students are normally expected to develop, 
implement, demonstrate, test, and evaluate a prototype, system, technical solution, experimental setup, 
or other project output appropriate to the approved project scope. 
 
The scope of the project should be sufficiently substantial to demonstrate the knowledge, skills, 
problem-solving ability, and independence expected of a Final Year Project, while remaining 
realistically achievable within the two-semester project period and the resources reasonably available 
to the group. 
 
The project scope and objectives may be refined during the course as students gain a better 
understanding of the problem, technical constraints, available resources, and results obtained. Any 
significant changes should be discussed with the project supervisor and appropriately documented. 
 
Project Initiation and Topic Development 
Following group formation, students are expected to collaboratively propose a project topic and 
conduct preliminary investigation before finalising the project direction. 
 
The initial project proposal should establish, as far as reasonably possible at that stage: 
– the problem or need to be addressed; 
– the background and significance of the problem; 
– the intended project goals and objectives; 
– the proposed solution or general technical direction; 
– preliminary feasibility considerations; 
– expected project outputs;

– major resources required; 
– responsibilities among group members; and 
– an initial project schedule. 
 
Project ideas should be supported by an appropriate preliminary investigation rather than based solely 
on assumptions. Students should consider relevant existing systems, products, technologies, theories, 
methods, technical literature, and other sources appropriate to the nature of the project. 
 
The proposed topic and scope will be discussed with the project supervisor. Students may be required 
to refine the problem definition, objectives, technical approach, project scale, or expected output to 
ensure that the proposed work is feasible and appropriate for the course. 
 
Project Development Process 
Although individual projects may follow different development processes, CCIT4080 projects should 
normally demonstrate logical progression from problem investigation to development and 
evaluation. Students should be able to show how major engineering decisions and project activities 
relate to the original problem and project objectives. 
 
The following components are generally expected. 
– Problem Investigation and Background Study 
Students should first develop a sufficiently detailed understanding of the problem being 
addressed, including its context, causes, users or stakeholders where applicable, existing 
constraints, and the reasons why a solution or an improvement is needed. 
 
Relevant statements and assumptions should, where appropriate, be supported by credible 
evidence such as academic literature, technical documentation, industry information, market 
information, standards, experimental observations, or other appropriate sources. 
 
– Review of Existing Knowledge and Solutions 
Students should investigate relevant theories, technologies, methods, designs, systems, products, 
services, or other existing solutions related to the project. 
The purpose of the review is not merely to describe what already exists. Students should analyse 
and compare relevant approaches, identify their strengths and limitations, and use the findings 
to inform the requirements and technical decisions of their own project. 
 
Depending on the nature of the project, this review may include academic literature, existing 
commercial products or services, engineering designs, algorithms, hardware components, 
software frameworks, technical standards, or other relevant material.

– Requirements and Success Criteria 
Based on the problem investigation and review, students should establish appropriate 
requirements for the proposed solution. These may include: 
⚫ 
Functional requirements, describing what the system or solution is expected to do; and 
⚫ 
Non-functional requirements, describing relevant performance, reliability, usability, safety, 
cost, resource, compatibility, or other constraints. 
 
Requirements should be sufficiently clear and, where reasonably possible, measurable and 
testable. Students should also establish appropriate success criteria, test conditions, performance 
metrics, or other means to determine whether the project objectives and requirements have been 
achieved. 
 
– Design 
The Design describes what solution is proposed and how it is intended to work. 
 
Students should develop an appropriate overall system or solution architecture and explain how 
its major components, subsystems, modules, or processes interact. Appropriate engineering 
diagrams and technical representations should be used where applicable, such as system 
architecture diagrams, circuit diagrams, flowcharts, state diagrams, mechanical drawings, 
interface diagrams, data-flow diagrams, or software structures. 
 
Major design decisions should be technically justified. Where reasonable alternatives exist, 
students should demonstrate that appropriate options have been considered and explain why the 
selected approach is suitable for the project's objectives, requirements, constraints, and available 
resources. 
 
– Methodology 
The Methodology describes how the project will be developed, investigated, verified, and 
evaluated. 
 
Students should explain the approaches and methods adopted to carry out the project and, where 
appropriate, compare alternative approaches before justifying the selected method. 
 
Depending on the project, the methodology may include development strategies, experimental 
procedures, data-collection methods, calibration methods, algorithm-development approaches, 
prototyping procedures, verification methods, testing strategies, and evaluation criteria.

The methodology should therefore provide a clear link between the project objectives and the 
methods used to determine whether those objectives have been achieved. 
 
– Implementation and Prototype Development 
The Implementation describes what has been developed, constructed, programmed, 
configured, integrated, or tested. 
 
Students are expected to translate the proposed design into a working prototype, system, solution, 
or other approved project output. The implementation should demonstrate meaningful technical 
work and appropriate application of engineering knowledge and skills. 
 
Students should document important stages of development, including significant technical 
decisions, modifications, difficulties encountered, troubleshooting performed, and solutions 
adopted. 
 
Where appropriate, evidence of implementation may include photographs, screenshots, source-
code records, circuit measurements, experimental records, fabrication outputs, version histories, 
demonstration records, or other relevant project artefacts. 
 
– Testing, Evaluation, and Refinement 
Testing and evaluation are essential parts of the project rather than activities to be performed 
only after development has finished. 
 
Students should develop suitable test cases, experiments, measurement procedures, or other 
evaluation methods based on the project's objectives, requirements, and success criteria. Testing 
should, where applicable: 
⚫ 
identify what is being tested; 
⚫ 
describe the test conditions and procedures; 
⚫ 
define the expected or acceptance criteria; 
⚫ 
record the results obtained; and 
⚫ 
compare the actual results with the intended objectives or requirements. 
 
Students should analyse unexpected results, limitations, failures, and discrepancies rather than 
simply reporting successful outcomes. 
 
Findings from testing and evaluation should be used to refine the design or implementation 
where appropriate. The final project should therefore demonstrate not only that a solution was 
produced, but also that its performance and limitations were critically evaluated.

– Project Management and Continuous Development 
Effective project management is an important part of CCIT4080. Students are expected to 
organise tasks, allocate responsibilities, manage resources, monitor risks, and maintain 
appropriate progress throughout the two-semester project period. 
 
The project schedule should be treated as a working project-management tool rather than merely 
an assessment requirement. Students should regularly compare planned progress with actual 
progress, identify delays or changes, explain significant differences, and revise the schedule or 
allocation of resources where appropriate. 
 
Similarly, responsibilities among group members should be sufficiently clear to allow both the 
group and the supervisor to identify individual contributions and monitor whether project work 
is progressing effectively. 
 
Assessment of the project therefore considers the development process and continual progress, 
rather than only the quality of the final deliverable. Extended periods of inactivity, insufficient 
evidence of development, or substantial work being postponed until immediately before 
assessment deadlines may negatively affect the relevant assessments even if a final prototype is 
eventually produced. 
 
Students should maintain appropriate records throughout the project to support project 
management, communication, assessment, and subsequent reporting.

(3) Project Group Formation 
 
Group Size and Project Expectations 
The standard project group size is 3 to 5 members. This group size serves as the normal benchmark 
for the project scope, workload, assessment requirements, and expected level of contribution. 
 
Where a group has only one to two members after the group-formation process, it will normally be 
expected to meet the same academic and technical standards and assessment requirements as 
groups of the standard size. A smaller group does not automatically receive a reduction in project 
scope, workload, assessment requirements, or marking expectations. 
 
Groups of more than five members are generally not encouraged and require approval from the 
instructor and Course Coordinator. Where such a group is approved, the project scope, technical depth, 
amount of development and testing, and division of responsibilities should reasonably reflect the 
additional manpower available to the group. 
 
Each member, regardless of group size, is expected to make a fair, meaningful, and identifiable 
contribution to the project. 
 
Group-Size Mark Adjustment 
To account for the potential additional manpower available to groups exceeding the standard group 
size, a group-size mark adjustment will normally be applied. 
 
For each additional member beyond five, 5 percentage points will be deducted from each 
member's final course mark, based on a 100-mark course score. 
 
For example: 
– 5 members: no adjustment 
– 6 members: −5 percentage points 
– 7 members: −10 percentage points 
 
The adjustment applies to the overall course mark, rather than to any individual assessment 
component. For example, a final course mark of 78/100 would become 73/100 after a 5-percentage-
point adjustment.

Group Formation Process 
Students are expected to form their own project groups during the first two lessons. 
 
Within one week after the end of Lesson 2, students are required to submit their draft group formation 
to the instructor. 
 
Students who have not yet formed or joined a group by the deadline remain responsible for making 
the necessary arrangements themselves. The instructor and Course Coordinator will not assign 
students to groups, combine groups, or determine group membership on students' behalf. 
 
The finalised group list will normally be announced before Lesson 3. Once the group has been formed, 
students are expected to sit and work together during the designated meeting sessions.  
 
Once confirmed, students should not independently add, remove, or exchange group members. Any 
subsequent change in group membership must be discussed with and approved by the instructor 
and/or Course Coordinator. 
 
Role Allocation and Division of Labour 
– Group Representative 
Each group must select a Group Representative who will serve as the primary contact 
between the group and the project supervisor. 
 
The Group Representative is primarily responsible for facilitating communication between the 
group and the supervisor, including conveying relevant information and communicating with the 
supervisor on the group's behalf. 
 
The role of Group Representative does not necessarily imply the role of a group leader, nor 
does it give the representative greater authority over other group members. Groups may organise 
their internal leadership, coordination, and task management in a manner appropriate to their 
project, and these responsibilities may be undertaken by the Group Representative or by other 
member(s). 
 
All group members remain responsible for their own participation, assigned work, awareness of 
course requirements and deadlines, and contribution to the overall project. 
 
– Division of Labour 
Group members should establish clear roles and responsibilities according to the nature of the 
project, the work required, and the abilities and strengths of individual members.

The allocation of work should be appropriately documented and updated where necessary 
through project records such as the project schedule, Logbook, meeting records, progress reports,

A balanced contribution does not necessarily mean an equal division of workload. Different 
members may undertake varying types and amounts of work depending on their roles, technical

Although responsibilities may be divided among members, students should maintain a

Individual contribution will be considered through the relevant individual assessment 
components, project-session observations, Peer Evaluation(s), Self-Reflection Report, project 
records, and other appropriate evidence as specified in the assessment arrangements. 
 
Supervisor Involvement 
The project supervisor will provide consultative guidance and monitor the overall progress of the 
project. 
 
The supervisor may provide feedback on matters such as project scope, feasibility, technical direction, 
project management, implementation, testing, and evaluation. However, consistent with the project-
based nature of CCIT4080, the supervisor's role is primarily advisory rather than instructional. 
 
Students are expected to investigate technical problems, acquire necessary knowledge and skills, 
consider alternative approaches, and make appropriate engineering decisions with increasing 
independence.

(4) Bi-Weekly Project Sessions 
 
The bi-weekly project sessions provide regular contact time for project development, group 
collaboration, supervisor consultation, and progress review. Students are expected to make 
productive use of the entire scheduled session according to the current needs and stage of their project. 
 
As stated in Section (1), the scheduled project sessions represent only part of the time required for 
the project. Students are expected to undertake substantial project work independently outside these 
sessions and use the scheduled sessions to continue development, coordinate with group members, 
review progress, and obtain supervisory feedback. 
 
Attendance 
Attendance at the bi-weekly project sessions is mandatory. 
 
Students are responsible for recording their attendance using the method(s) provided by the instructor 
and the College's attendance system. Attendance records will be used to monitor participation 
throughout the course. 
 
Students are expected to attend the entire scheduled project session. Prolonged lateness, early 
departure, or absence may result in a deduction from the final course mark. In particular, 
attendance below 70% throughout the course, without a valid reason and appropriate 
supporting documentation, will result in a significant mark deduction. 
 
The consultation period allocated to each group represents only part of the scheduled project session. 
Students who attend only their group's consultation period—for example, by arriving shortly before 
the consultation and leaving immediately afterwards—will normally be considered absent from that 
project session. 
 
Participation and Collaboration 
Students are expected to participate actively throughout the project sessions. Attendance alone does 
not necessarily demonstrate meaningful participation. 
 
Depending on the nature and current stage of the project, appropriate activities may include: 
– discussing and reviewing project progress with group members; 
– coordinating tasks and responsibilities; 
– conducting research or technical investigation; 
– developing or refining system designs; 
– programming, constructing, integrating, or troubleshooting project components;

– analysing data or test results;

Supervisor consultation may include: 
– reporting and reviewing current project progress;

– reviewing designs, prototypes, implementation, or testing; 
– discussing problems or difficulties encountered; 
– considering alternative approaches or possible improvements; 
– reviewing project schedules and upcoming activities; and 
– receiving feedback and guidance on subsequent project work. 
 
Groups should prepare appropriately for consultation so that the available time can be used effectively. 
Students should, wherever possible, present specific work, evidence, questions, difficulties, or 
decisions for discussion rather than providing only general statements about progress.

scheduled project sessions (6 sessions per semester), and carries 3 credits. 
 
The project is developed continuously across both semesters. The scheduled project sessions, 
assessment milestones, and submission deadlines are intended to support the progressive 
development of the project from initial problem investigation and proposal, through design and 
implementation, to testing, evaluation, and final presentation of the completed work. 
 
Students are responsible for monitoring the course schedule, SOUL announcements, and any 
subsequent updates issued by the Course Coordinator or project supervisor.

General Assessment Principles 
CCIT4080 assesses both the project development process and the resulting project outcomes. 
Assessment is therefore not based solely on whether a final prototype or product has been completed. 
 
Depending on the assessment component, consideration may be given to: 
– understanding and investigation of the problem; 
– review and appropriate use of relevant existing knowledge, technologies, systems, products, or 
solutions; 
– definition of meaningful and testable project objectives and requirements; 
– appropriateness and justification of engineering and technical decisions; 
– quality and depth of system or solution design; 
– implementation and technical development; 
– project progress throughout the two-semester period; 
– testing, evaluation, analysis, and refinement of the project; 
– quality and functionality of the final prototype, solution, or other approved project output; 
– project planning, scheduling, and resource management; 
– quality and professionalism of technical documentation and presentation; 
– teamwork, participation, and communication; and 
– identifiable individual contribution. 
 
Students are expected to support significant claims concerning project progress, implementation, 
performance, testing, or achievement with appropriate evidence. Depending on the nature of the 
project, such evidence may include technical diagrams, calculations, source-code records, 
photographs, videos, screenshots, prototype records, test procedures, experimental data, test results, 
version histories, project records, or other appropriate artefacts. 
 
The quality of the evidence and analysis is important. Merely stating that a feature has been completed, 
a system is effective, a project is "on schedule", or an objective has been achieved does not by itself 
demonstrate the claim. 
 
Where an assessment requires review of project progress, students should distinguish clearly among: 
– completed work; 
– work currently in progress; and 
– planned or remaining work. 
 
Similarly, where scheduling or resource allocation is assessed, students should review planned 
versus actual progress or usage, explain significant differences, and revise subsequent plans where 
appropriate.

(6) Assessment Details 
 
General Assessment Requirements 
SOUL is the primary platform for formal assessment submission. Students are responsible for 
ensuring that the correct files are submitted in the required format and by the applicable deadline 
specified in Section (5) and/or subsequent course announcements. 
 
In addition, each group must maintain the shared OneDrive folder described in Section (5) for project 
archiving, supervisor access, and second-examiner access where applicable. 
 
The assessment descriptions in this section provide guidance on the expected scope and content of 
each assessment. They should be read together with the corresponding marking rubrics. The 
suggested headings or structures are not necessarily rigid templates; depending on the nature of the 
project, students may organise their work differently where an alternative structure communicates the 
required content more appropriately. 
 
Assessment Based on Content and Evidence 
Assessment is based on the quality and relevance of the work actually presented, rather than 
merely the presence of a section heading, diagram, table, prototype feature, or other item. 
 
Where a required or assessed element is materially absent, the corresponding marking category may 
be assessed within the Inadequate band. Merely including a heading without meaningful relevant 
content does not constitute completion of that element. 
 
Students should ensure that significant claims concerning project design, implementation, progress, 
functionality, performance, testing, or achievement are supported by appropriate evidence wherever 
applicable. 
 
Evidence may include, depending on the nature of the project: 
– academic or technical references; 
– engineering calculations; 
– system, circuit, mechanical, software, or other technical diagrams; 
– photographs or videos of development work; 
– screenshots or screen recordings; 
– source-code or version-control records; 
– experimental procedures and observations; 
– test cases, measurement data, and test results; 
– project schedules and progress records; 
– meeting records and Logbook entries; and 
– other relevant project artefacts. 
 
Claims that cannot be reasonably supported by the submitted work or other available project evidence 
may receive limited credit.

Accuracy and Consistency of Project Reporting 
Assessment materials should accurately represent the actual status of the project. Students should 
clearly distinguish between: 
– work that has been completed; 
– work that is currently in progress; and 
– work that is planned but not yet completed. 
 
Planned features, intended experiments, expected results, or proposed improvements should not be 
presented as completed achievements. 
 
Information should also remain reasonably consistent across different parts of the project 
documentation. Where the project scope, technical approach, requirements, schedule, resources, or 
implementation has changed, the change should be clearly explained and the relevant assessment 
materials updated accordingly. 
 
Use of Appropriate Sources and References 
Students are expected to use appropriate and credible sources to support background information, 
technical decisions, engineering claims, component specifications, market comparisons, 
methodologies, and other externally derived information where relevant. 
 
The quality, relevance, and appropriate use of sources may affect the assessment of the corresponding 
content. 
 
All sources used should be cited consistently. APA and IEEE citation styles are preferred for this 
course, although another recognised academic citation style may be used consistently where 
appropriate. 
 
Group and Individual Assessment 
CCIT4080 includes both group-based and individual assessments. 
 
Group-based marks reflect the overall quality of the work produced collaboratively, whereas 
individual marks consider the student's own contribution, involvement, technical work, 
communication, and other relevant evidence. 
 
Students within the same group should therefore not assume that identical final marks will necessarily 
be awarded where meaningful differences in individual contribution or performance are identified. 
Relevant evidence may include: 
– observations during project sessions; 
– work demonstrated during supervisor consultation; 
– submitted project materials; 
– Logbook and project records; 
– Peer Evaluation(s); 
– Individual Self-Reflection Reports; 
– presentation performance; and 
– other appropriate evidence of individual contribution.

Project Proposal Presentation (5%) 
The Project Proposal Presentation represents the first formal assessment of the proposed project. Its 
purpose is to demonstrate that the group has identified an appropriate problem, conducted sufficient 
preliminary investigation, developed a feasible preliminary solution, and established a realistic plan 
for carrying out the project over the two-semester period. 
 
Each group should normally allocate approximately 15 minutes for the presentation. 
 
The presentation should be prepared for an engineering audience and should communicate the 
proposed project clearly and professionally. Supporting references, diagrams, preliminary 
calculations, comparisons, or other evidence should be used where appropriate. 
 
The presentation should address the following areas. 
– Problem Statement, Project Goals and Objectives 
Clearly define the real-world problem, engineering need, or technical challenge that the project 
intends to address. 
 
The problem should be sufficiently specific and should be supported, where appropriate, by 
credible evidence such as academic publications, technical reports, industry information, market 
information, standards, statistics, or other relevant sources. 
 
The group should explain: 
⚫ 
what the identified problem is; 
⚫ 
who or what is affected by the problem, where relevant; 
⚫ 
why the problem is meaningful or worth addressing; 
⚫ 
the overall goal of the proposed project; and 
⚫ 
the specific objectives that the project intends to achieve. 
 
Project objectives should be sufficiently clear to guide subsequent development. Where 
reasonably possible, objectives should be measurable or evaluable at the end of the project. 
 
The group should therefore begin considering appropriate success criteria, performance 
indicators, test cases, measurements, or other methods that could later be used to determine 
whether the objectives have been achieved. 
 
– Project Feasibility and Proposed Solution 
Present the proposed solution and explain how it is expected to address the identified problem 
and achieve the project objectives. 
 
The presentation should demonstrate that the group has conducted sufficient preliminary 
investigation to support the proposed direction rather than selecting technologies or approaches 
without justification. 
 
Where appropriate, the group should consider: 
⚫ 
the overall system or solution concept; 
⚫ 
major hardware and/or software components;

relevant engineering principles, technologies, or methods; 
⚫

expected cost; 
⚫ 
technical complexity; 
⚫

Free-Rider Reporting and Intervention 
A free rider generally refers to a group member who makes substantially insufficient contribution 
to the group project while benefiting from work completed by the other members. 
 
Peer Evaluation provides one mechanism for documenting concerns about unequal contribution. 
However, because a formal free-rider case may lead to substantial consequences for an individual 
student, Peer Evaluation alone is not sufficient to initiate formal intervention. 
 
Formal Reporting Requirements 
Where group members wish the project supervisor and/or Course Coordinator to formally 
investigate and intervene in a suspected free-rider case, both of the following are required: 
⚫ 
The concern must be recorded through the relevant Peer Evaluation(s); and 
⚫ 
A formal email must be sent to the supervisor using the student's College email account. 
 
Both requirements should normally be satisfied. A concern mentioned only in a Peer Evaluation, 
Individual Self-Reflection Report, verbal discussion, instant message, or informal communication 
will not normally be treated as a formal request for free-rider intervention. 
 
The formal email should identify the concern clearly and provide sufficient information for the 
supervisor to understand the situation. Where possible, relevant evidence should also be provided 
or identified. Examples of supporting evidence may include: 
⚫ 
agreed task allocations; 
⚫ 
meeting minutes or records; 
⚫ 
Logbook entries; 
⚫ 
communication records; 
⚫ 
source-code or version-control histories; 
⚫ 
shared-document revision histories; 
⚫ 
photographs or screenshots; 
⚫ 
records of missed agreed work or deadlines; 
⚫ 
evidence of repeated non-participation; and 
⚫ 
other relevant project records. 
 
Timely Reporting 
Concerns should be raised as soon as a persistent contribution problem becomes apparent. 
 
Students should not wait until the final assessment period to report a problem that has existed for 
a substantial period of time, particularly where earlier reporting could reasonably have allowed the 
supervisor to review the situation and provide guidance or intervention. 
 
Reporting a concern does not automatically establish that another student is a free rider. The 
supervisor and/or Course Coordinator will review the information available and may seek 
explanations or additional evidence from the students concerned before determining whether 
further action is appropriate.
