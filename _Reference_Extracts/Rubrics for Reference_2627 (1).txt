CCIT4080 as the Final Year Project 
CCIT4080 Project on Knowledge Product Development is regarded as the Final Year Project of 
the Associate of Engineering (AENG) programme. 
 
The assessments in this course are therefore intended to demonstrate the comprehensive knowledge 
and skills accumulated from previous courses, together with new knowledge and skills acquired 
independently during the project. 
 
Students should therefore expect the academic and technical standards of this course to be 
comparatively high. The project should demonstrate not merely the completion of assigned tasks, but 
also the ability to integrate knowledge, investigate unfamiliar problems, make appropriate 
engineering decisions, develop and evaluate solutions, and communicate the resulting work 
professionally. 
 
The course is intended to prepare students for further education and professional careers, 
where autonomy, the ability to address complex challenges, independent learning, and the 
ability to meet high standards are essential. 
 
Students are expected to approach their project diligently, apply their accumulated knowledge 
appropriately, acquire additional knowledge and skills where necessary, and demonstrate the ability 
to address real-world engineering problems.

Independent Project Work 
Students should not limit their project work to designated contact hours (i.e., the scheduled 
project meetings with the supervisor). 
 
As a general reference for study workload, many courses expect approximately two hours of study 
outside scheduled contact hours for every contact hour, although the actual amount varies with the 
course's nature and difficulty. For CCIT4080, the amount of self-directed project work should be no 
less than that normally expected in other courses. 
 
Students and groups are expected to make continuous progress independently between scheduled 
project meetings. Contact hours are primarily intended for: 
• 
reporting and demonstrating progress to the project supervisor;  
• 
receiving feedback and consultation;  
• 
discussing problems and possible solutions;  
• 
conducting meaningful group discussions and collaborative work; and  
• 
facilitating face-to-face group meetings where arranging additional common times may be 
difficult because of different course timetables.  
 
Scheduled project sessions should therefore not be treated as the only time during which project 
development takes place.

Use of Resources and Generative Artificial Intelligence 
Students are encouraged to make effective use of available resources, including libraries, laboratories, 
equipment, software tools, technical documentation, Generative Artificial Intelligence (GenAI)-
enabled tools, and consultation with their project supervisor. 
 
The availability of GenAI and other digital tools can support activities such as information retrieval, 
idea generation, writing, coding, troubleshooting, and documentation. However, students remain fully 
responsible for the accuracy, technical validity, appropriateness, originality, and academic 
integrity of the work submitted. 
 
Given the increasing availability and convenience of GenAI-assisted writing and other forms of 
support, students should understand that expectations for the quality of documentation, reporting, 
technical evidence, and presentation are correspondingly higher. 
 
Well-written or visually polished material alone does not constitute Excellent performance. Higher 
performance bands require substantive evidence of engineering work, such as appropriate 
technical depth, justified design decisions, implementation, testing and validation, critical 
evaluation, and demonstrated understanding of the submitted work. 
 
All use of AI tools must comply with the applicable College and course requirements concerning 
academic integrity, acknowledgement, and declaration of AI-assisted work.

Reference Standard for the Highest Performance Bands 
Starting from the 2026/27 academic year, the course will adopt, on a trial basis, an enhanced 
reference standard when interpreting the upper performance bands. 
 
As CCIT4080 serves as the Final Year Project of the AENG programme, project work associated 
with performance in the A range, particularly A+, would generally be expected to demonstrate an 
overall quality comparable to a project that could reasonably be presented or submitted to an external 
student competition, project showcase, academic event, or equivalent professional setting. 
 
Participation in an external competition or event is not required. The comparison is intended only 
as a reference benchmark for the expected level of excellence. 
 
Depending on the nature of the project, this may be reflected through the overall combination of 
technical achievement, completeness, originality, appropriate engineering complexity, independent 
learning, functionality, systematic testing and validation, quality of evidence, evaluation of results, 
limitations and engineering trade-offs, documentation, and professional presentation. 
 
Research-oriented projects or projects that are not naturally suited to external competitions may 
demonstrate an equivalent level through the quality of their methodology, technical investigation, 
experimentation, analysis, validation, and academic or professional presentation. 
 
A functioning prototype or completion of the basic stated requirements should therefore not, by itself, 
be interpreted as sufficient evidence of Excellent performance. 
 
At the same time, a project's apparent suitability for an external competition does not automatically 
result in an A or A+ grade. Performance will still be evaluated against the relevant assessment criteria 
and the overall evidence submitted.

Group Performance and Individual Performance 
Students should note that members of the same project group are not necessarily awarded the 
same final course grade. 
 
A substantial proportion of the project is collaborative and is therefore assessed at group level. 
However, the course also evaluates individual performance through individual assessment 
components and evidence of individual contribution accumulated throughout the course. 
 
Relevant evidence may include: 
• 
individual technical contribution; 
• 
contribution to reports, documentation, presentations, prototypes, testing, and other 
deliverables; 
• 
completion or non-submission of required individual assessments; 
• 
quality and consistency of individual work; 
• 
evidence of tasks completed independently; 
• 
ability to explain, demonstrate, and defend one's own contribution; 
• 
progress demonstrated during project meetings; 
• 
meaningful attendance and participation; 
• 
preparedness for discussions with the project supervisor; 
• 
participation in problem-solving and project decision-making; 
• 
communication and cooperation within the group; 
• 
Peer Evaluations; 
• 
Individual Self-Reflection Reports; and 
• 
other relevant evidence available to the project supervisor. 
 
Attendance alone does not constitute meaningful participation. Students are expected to make 
productive use of the project sessions, contribute to discussions, report progress, respond to questions, 
and where appropriate, provide evidence of their work, such as designs, calculations, drafts, code, 
photographs, videos, prototype demonstrations, test results, or other project outputs. 
 
To comply with College and course requirements, prolonged lateness, early departure, or repeated 
absence will result in penalties in accordance with the applicable College and course 
requirements.

Students with valid reasons for absence should follow the applicable College procedures and provide 
supporting documentation where required. 
 
Where clear and sustained differences in individual performance are supported by the available 
assessment evidence, students within the same group may receive different overall results. Such 
differences in assessed performance may result in final grades differing by approximately one 
to two sub-grades, where justified by the evidence and the marks obtained across the relevant 
assessment components. 
 
Where no meaningful difference in individual contribution or performance can be identified, students 
within the same group would normally be expected to receive similar results for components based 
principally on the same group work. 
 
Individual differentiation is, therefore, evidence-based and is not an automatic adjustment applied 
to every group.
